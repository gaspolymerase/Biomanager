User guide

Using BioManager

Everything you need to get your lab into BioManager and keep it there — from the first launch to a whole lab on a shared server. Read it top to bottom once, or jump to what you need.

1 · Getting started

Install

Download BioManager for your computer from the download page.

macOSUnzip, drag BioManager into Applications. The first time, right-click it → Open, then Open.
WindowsExtract the zip, open the BioManager folder, run BioManager.exe. If Windows warns you, choose More info → Run anyway.
LinuxMark BioManager-Linux.AppImage as executable (right-click → Properties, or chmod +x) and open it.
AndroidThe phone app connects to a lab server. See Phones.
Why the warning? BioManager isn't signed by Apple or Microsoft, so the system asks you to confirm once. It never asks again for that version.

Set up your lab

The first time BioManager opens, you create your account — the first account is the admin — and BioManager asks a few questions about your lab. Nothing is set up until you answer, so you only get the databases you'll actually use.

  1. What does your lab keep? Tick what you work with: mice, zebrafish, flies, worms, something else, plasmids, samples, orders, reagents, antibodies.
  2. A few details for each. What to call it (e.g. Mouse room B); for the mouse colony, how many racks the room has, their size and how positions are labelled (A1, 1-1 or 1, 2, 3…); for flies and worms, which incubator temperatures you run. Leave anything blank for the usual — it can all be changed later.
  3. Which lab functions, and what members may do. The calendar and notebook, and whether members may add databases of their own.
  4. Done. BioManager creates exactly those databases — the racks and incubators too — and takes you Home, where Getting started shows what to do next.

Need something later? Add database in the sidebar adds another at any time. An admin switches databases on or off under Setup in the sidebar; switching one off hides it without deleting anything.

People who join later get a one-page welcome tour of the lab's databases the first time they sign in.

The Home page

Home is where each day starts. It shows only what needs attention:

  • mice older than 30 weeks, upcoming weanings and the genotyping queue;
  • fly vials and worm plates due to be flipped or chunked, and progeny to collect;
  • reagents that have expired, expire soon or are running low;
  • zebrafish mating tanks due back, the next 14 days of the calendar and recent orders.

While you're new, Home also shows Getting started: the first steps for what your lab keeps — add a rack, bring in your mice, print cage cards, set up your fly incubators, invite your lab. Each step ticks itself off when it's done, and Hide this list puts it away. Help at the bottom of the sidebar opens this guide.

2 · Everyday basics

Every database works the same way, so once you know one you know them all.

Sheets you edit in place
Click a cell and type. It saves as you go — there is no Save button. A green dot at the start of a row means the record is active (a living animal, a vial in use).
Find things
The search box above each sheet filters it; the chips beside it (All, Alive, Mine…) narrow it further. ⌘/Ctrl + K searches everything, from anywhere.
Tabs
Every page you open becomes a tab along the top. Drag to reorder, middle-click to close, Alt + 1…9 to switch.
Add many
Describe one record and say how many — 4 females, 2 males — or upload a CSV. You get an editable preview first, with the IDs that will be assigned. Fill down copies a value into the empty cells below; Skip leaves a row out.
Act on many at once
Tick rows (shift-click selects a range) and a bar rises from the bottom: set a field, move, add to an experiment, sac. Only records you may edit are changed; the rest are counted and skipped.
Undo
Every bulk action is listed under Batches in the sidebar and can be undone. If someone has changed those records since, BioManager asks before overwriting their edit.
Export and print
Export downloads what you see as CSV; Print gives a clean printout of the sheet.
History
Every change is recorded with who made it and what changed. Admins read it under Audit log.

3 · Mouse colony

The colony has tabs across the top: Mice, Cages, Litters, Breeders, Experiments, Strains and Presets.

Racks first

Open Cages → Rack grid → New rack. Give it rows and columns and choose how positions are labelled — match the stickers on your real racks (D7, 4-7, 7D, or 1 to 80). Each rack keeps its own scheme.

Add your mice

  1. Add your lines under Strains.
  2. On Mice, choose Add many. Describe a group, or upload your old spreadsheet as a CSV — columns called sex, dob, cage, litter and notes are recognised, anything else is ignored. Leave out mouse IDs: BioManager assigns them in order.
  3. Type new in the cage column to put the whole group in one new cage.
  4. Check the preview, then save. Give each cage a purpose (breeding, stock, experiments) and a rack position on Cages, or drag it on the rack grid.

Litters and weaning

When a litter is born, open the breeding cage and choose Litter born. BioManager works out the weaning date (P21) and the genotyping date (about P28); both appear on Home and the calendar when they come due. At weaning, use Wean and distribute pups, or add the pups with Add many and move them to their cages.

Genotyping, experiments, sac

  • Mice with the status geno wait in the genotyping queue on Home. Enter results in the transgene columns.
  • To start an experiment, tick the mice, choose Add to experiment and name the treatment group.
  • Setting a date of death (or Sac from the selection bar) ends a mouse's record; it stays in the history.
  • Breeders older than 30 weeks are flagged on Breeders and Home.

Mine, shared, everyone

The My colony / Shared / Everyone switch changes what you see. What you may edit doesn't change: your own records, breeding cages and anything marked shared. See Working as a lab.

4 · Zebrafish

  • Lines — New line with its ZFIN name and owner.
  • Tanks — New tank, then New fish to put a group in it with a headcount. A tank can later be split into named individuals.
  • Systems — Add system for each recirculating system, and Log reading for water quality.
  • Matings — Set up a mating, say when the fish go back, and mark the tank Returned afterwards. Mating tanks due back show on Home.
  • Clutches — New clutch for each fertilisation; Log sac keeps the sac log.

5 · Flies and worms

Drosophila keeps vials in racks inside incubators; C. elegans keeps plates in boxes.

  1. Under Incubators & racks, add your incubators with their temperatures, then racks inside them.
  2. Add vials with New vial: genotype and purpose — stock, experiment, cross or progeny.
  3. For a cross, fill in both sides. BioManager schedules egg collections and tells you when progeny will be adults.
  4. Flip (or chunk) schedules follow temperature: set the days per temperature in Settings, and a rack moved from 25 °C to 18 °C changes its schedule by itself. What's due shows on Home and under Schedule.
  5. Worms have a frozen-stock list: New frozen lot records a freeze and its thaw tests.

6 · Other organisms

Add database in the sidebar builds a database for anything else you keep — Xenopus, axolotls, cell lines, yeast. Start from a preset or a blank sheet and answer:

  • The words it uses — tank, vial or plate; strain, line or stock; litter, clutch or batch. The whole interface then uses your words.
  • How it's counted — one record per animal, groups with a headcount, or both.
  • What it needs — crosses, cohorts, a nursery stage, genotyping, environment logs, frozen stocks, census…
  • Schedules — e.g. "wean at P21", which may depend on temperature.
  • Your own columns — text, numbers, dates, dropdowns, people or links.

You can change any of it later under the database's Settings, without losing records.

7 · Plasmids

  • Add plasmids one at a time, or several with Add many.
  • Open a plasmid and upload a GenBank, FASTA or SnapGene file: the sequence and its features appear on an interactive map you can edit.
  • Plasmid boxes live on the same rack grid as everything else, so every tube has a position.

8 · Samples, orders, reagents, antibodies

Samples
Tissue and material, linked to the animal it came from, with where it's stored and the box position.
Orders
Switch to Board and drag an order from requested to ordered to received. Keep vendor, catalogue number, price and grant.
Reagents
Quantity, concentration, hazard, lot and expiry. Expired and expiring items show on Home.
Antibodies
Host, clone, conjugate, dilution, RRID and where each vial is.

Each inventory separates your own stock from the lab's common shelf. Admins change columns, statuses and categories under Configure; renaming a status relabels its items.

9 · Calendar and notebook

  • The calendar shows your events and to-dos with colony dates — weanings, genotyping, sac reminders — filled in for you. Calendars connects Google Calendar or any ICS feed.
  • The notebook keeps pages in tabs, with templates for pages you write often, images and attachments. Link a page to animals and events so each points to the other.
  • Utilities has molecular weights and a concentration-to-mass calculator.

10 · Cage cards and phones

Cage cards

On Cages, choose Cage cards → Print. Cards are sized for standard cage-card stock (vial and tank labels too) and carry a QR code. On a lab server, scanning a card opens that cage — ready to edit.

Phones

Phones connect to your lab's BioManager server, so everyone sees the same data.

  • Android app: download BioManager-Android.apk from the download page and allow your browser to install it. Type your lab server's address once. The Scan button reads cage cards.
  • iPhone, or without the app: open the server in Safari or Chrome and choose Share → Add to Home Screen (iPhone) or ⋮ → Install app (Android). It opens like an app; your phone camera scans cage cards.
The desktop app on one computer is private to that computer — phones can't reach it. For phones, your lab needs a server.

11 · Working as a lab

Who can do what

  • You edit what you own — your mice, cages, vials and items.
  • Breeding cages and anything marked shared belong to the whole lab.
  • Admins can change anything, approve new members, and read the audit log.
  • Everyone sees everything, because a census with holes is not a census.

People joining and leaving

New sign-ups wait until an admin approves them in Settings → Manage users. When someone leaves, Overview lists every cage by owner, idle cages and living mice without a cage, and Racks & boxes hands their racks to someone else. Members can also sign in with Google or Microsoft if the server allows it.

A lab server

For a whole lab, BioManager runs on one server (a department VM or a lab machine) that everyone opens in a browser. It uses PostgreSQL, serves HTTPS, and backs itself up every night, testing a restore every week. A desktop database can be moved onto it in one step. Keep the server on your campus network, VPN or a private network such as Tailscale. Ask about setting one up.

12 · Your data and backups

macOS~/Library/Application Support/Biomanager/
Windows%APPDATA%\Biomanager\
Linux~/.local/share/Biomanager/
  • Your data lives outside the app, so updating or reinstalling never touches it.
  • Settings → Export my data downloads your records as a zip at any time.
  • Back up that folder with your usual backup (Time Machine, File History). Copy it while BioManager is closed.
Don't keep BioManager's data in Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive or iCloud Drive. Syncing corrupts database files. BioManager warns you if it finds itself in one.

13 · Troubleshooting

The app won't open on my Mac
Right-click it and choose Open. If macOS says it's damaged, move it to Applications first, then try again.
Linux: the AppImage does nothing
Mark it executable. If it still won't start, install libfuse2, or use the .tar.gz and run BioManager/BioManager.
Android: "Couldn't reach the server"
Check the address with your admin, and that your phone is on the lab's network or VPN. If your lab uses its own certificate, install it under Settings → Security.
I can't edit a record
It belongs to someone else and isn't shared. Ask them, or an admin.
I made a mistake with a bulk change
Batches in the sidebar → undo it.
Something else
Open an issue — say which system you use and what you expected to happen.
